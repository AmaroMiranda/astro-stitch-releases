How it works

You pick the panels. The app aligns and composes.

No layers, no masks, no editing walkthrough. The mode you choose already sets projection, stitching and cropping — and what you see on screen is what lands in the file.

Mosaic detail

The detail the wide view hides.

A 900 × 900 pixel crop of the exported file — the whole mosaic is 2169 × 2299 — resized here to fit the page.

Stitching does not live in the whole picture — it lives here, where two panels meet and the relief has to carry on from one into the other.

Mineral Moon

The colour was already there.

The Moon is not grey. Its soil changes chemistry from one region to the next, and that changes the colour: basalt rich in titanium reflects bluer, iron-rich soil pulls towards orange. Your camera does record that difference — it is just too faint for the eye to find on its own.

Mineral Moon amplifies what is already in the file, without touching brightness and without inventing colour. Below, the same crop of the same mosaic: on the left as it came out of the stitch, on the right with vibrance at 70% and colour noise reduction at maximum.

Measured across the disc, between the two outputs above: mean saturation rises from 1.31% to 6.35% — 4.9×. Brightness was not touched.

Four subjects

Each with its own geometry.

  • Moon

    Whole disc or close-up. It traces the limb angle by angle instead of cropping to a loose ellipse.

  • Sun

    The same disc geometry as the Moon, with the edge handling that solar granulation calls for.

  • Deep sky

    Star-based alignment and photometric equalisation between panels, so the sky background does not step.

  • Panorama

    Landscape on the ground, with spherical or planar projection according to the loop closure measured on your own panels.

Latest update

v0.56.2 — Less stepping on the Moon's limb

  • The step on the limb is nearly gone. In mosaics with many panels, the edge of the disc could jump right at the seam between two of them.

    On the test set, the jump dropped from 11.7 to 1.5 pixels.

  • More of the disc kept. With the panels landing in the right place, the Moon's outline is traced all the way round instead of in pieces.

    On a 16-panel phone mosaic, edge coverage rose from 47% to 64%.

Support the project

What it costs is time. Every fix in the release notes only ships after being measured on a test set, and it is the measuring — not the code — that takes the days. That is how the step on the limb went from 11.7 to 1.5 pixels instead of merely looking better.
