Release notes

Every fix here states its effect in one sentence, with the measurement that closed it right below. The number holds for the test set named — it is not a general promise about any image.

The Windows builds live on GitHub releases. The Android app is updated through Google Play.

v0.56.2 6 Oct 2026

Windows x64

Less stepping on the Moon's limb

The edge of the disc barely jumps between panels now. In mosaics with many panels the limb would drop, flatten out and drop again, right at the seams of the composition. The cause was a geometry error on the way from alignment into assembly: each panel was placed with an offset proportional to its scale and to its distance from the centre, reaching 10 pixels.

On the test set, the jump at the limb fell from 11.7 to 1.5 pixels.

More of the disc kept. With the panels landing in the right place, the Moon's outline is now traced all the way round instead of in pieces.

On a 16-panel phone mosaic, edge coverage rose from 47% to 64%, and the disc came out 2.4% larger — without changing the scale of the image.

The fix applies to Moon and Sun, and changes neither panorama nor deep sky.

v0.56.1 5 Oct 2026

Windows x64

The halo around the Moon is gone

Anyone composing a full Moon mosaic used to see a bright band around the disc, with a sharp edge against the background.

The diagnosis changed what the problem even was: the band was not data invented by the app. The limb profile was measured at three points — in the file coming out of the stacker, in the panel once aligned and processed, and in the final mosaic.

The light around the disc is in the file that goes in — it is the halo of the capture itself, 27 sigma above the sky noise. The app reproduced it faithfully up to +20 px and then cut: 24 levels in 10 px, where the capture would have gone on falling gently for another 100 px. The ring was visible because it had an edge, not because it had light.

What changed: the cut now dies off as a ramp, over 5% of the disc radius.

v0.56.0 4 Oct 2026

Windows x64

Disc cropping now follows the measured limb

Full Moon. The disc is no longer cropped by a loose ellipse; it now follows the measured outline of the limb, angle by angle — 1440 of them.

On the test set: the band falls from 0.112R present at 100% of angles to zero; the gradient from limb to black falls from 297 to 28 px; and not one pixel of the disc is lost.

An ellipse could never have fixed this: the limb varies by about ±10 px between angles, and an ellipse is a single curve — any margin wide enough not to bite into the disc at one point leaves a band at every other.

Partial phases (gibbous, crescent). The observed sky of each panel is now preserved, and the artificial band is gone. The background only turns black with the “Black background” option on.

An honest note: at reduced resolution the band shrinks a great deal but does not reach zero. The correct description is reduction of the band — zero at full resolution, residual at smaller scales.

Also in this version: the analysis progress bar no longer sits still and then jumps to the end — it now shows real progress, in four stages.
